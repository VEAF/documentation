    Persistent campaign¶

    One mission that runs on a server for weeks, in the spirit of Foothold: zones that change hands, a front that moves on its own, enemy air, credits to spend, and a save that resumes where the server stopped. Built on the VEAF tools and scripts alone, without Moose. It is the other mode of the multi-mission campaign: same campaign.yaml, same zones, same garrisons, but the campaign moves during the flight rather than between two missions.

    What the players see¶

    • The F10 map shows each zone as a circle in its owner's colour, with its name, the number of its waypoint and its garrison's state, and the connections between zones. An enemy zone reads "strength unknown" until it has been reconnoitred.
    • A zone never changes hands directly: when its garrison is destroyed it turns neutral (grey). A neutral zone is taken by holding the ground: ground units of a single side staying there for capture_seconds (120 s by default) — CTLD 2 troops or crate, a convoy, a _spawn group, a Combined Arms vehicle, a landed helicopter. Landed helicopters alone take helicopter_capture_seconds (600 s), and leave a token garrison: what a helicopter carries, helicopter_garrison soldiers (6) and no vehicle, whatever the zone's size class. The new garrison appears at once.
    • A pilot on the ground after an ejection is nobody's enemy: the survivor the CSAR module puts down neither holds nor contests a zone, wakes no zone and stops no convoy, in every VEAF script.
    • Slots follow the bases: an airfield taken goes to the side that took it, with its dynamic slots. Every airfield of the campaign has its slots ready from the build, even those the players' side does not hold yet (see Building the mission). The campaign alone decides who owns an airfield: DCS's own capture of an airbase by the units around it is turned off on every airfield of the map.
    • The front moves on its own: the enemy counter-attacks, reinforces its zones by helicopter, and sends CAP, strikes and bombers.
    • The enemy pays for what it sends with the resources its factories make: destroying its factories or cutting its supply network grounds its CAP in the end.
    • The map is tinted by side, each zone holding the territory nearest to it, and the front reads between the two colours.
    • Every announcement says who speaks: "Blue command:" (or red) for what a side does or suffers, "Intelligence:" for what is learnt of the enemy — a convoy or a helicopter leaving, a raid inbound.
    • The F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign menu gives the situation, the credits, my earnings, the tasks, the intelligence and the shop; the "(admin)" commands come last.
    • The game ends when one side holds no zone left: a victory if it is the enemy, a defeat if it is us. Everyone is told, and the campaign starts afresh at the next load of the mission.

    Declaring a persistent campaign¶

    It is a multi-mission campaign campaign.yaml with mode: persistent, and, when needed, a dynamic: block. The objectives and the number of missions play no part: winning is holding every zone.

    campaign:
  name: Georgian Front
  theatre: Caucasus
  era: MODERN
  mode: persistent
  player_side: blue

zones:
  - { name: Kobuleti, at: { airfield: Kobuleti }, size: airfield, side: blue, radius: 3000 }
  - { name: Poti, at: { lat: 42.155, lon: 41.705 }, size: outpost, side: neutral }
  - { name: Senaki, at: { airfield: Senaki-Kolkhi }, size: airfield, side: red, radius: 3000 }

connections:
  - [Kobuleti, Poti]
  - [Poti, Senaki]

dynamic:            # all optional: every key has its default, below
  wake_radius: 60000
  economy:
    start_credits: 500

    A complete example, ready to copy, ships with the tools: src/defaults/campaign-folder-persistent/campaign.yaml.

    Without a sides: block, a side keeps no reserve: its garrisons are drawn without limit. With a reserve, it empties as in a multi-mission campaign, and a side with nothing left only fields token garrisons.

    Building the mission¶

    .\veaf-tools.exe campaign init C:\Campaigns\Georgia
.\veaf-tools.exe campaign next C:\Campaigns\Georgia

    Then the mission of the missions\mission-01\mission folder is built like any mission, and put on the server. campaign next run again on the same folder updates it without touching the rest of the work done in it.

    Slots on every airfield of the campaign. campaign next writes, in the mission's src\warehouses.yaml, the players' side's airports: list with every airfield of the campaign, whatever its side at the start. Without that list, the build opens dynamic slots only on the airfields the side holds when the mission is built: an airfield captured in flight had no slot to offer (Minakh, 2026-11-19). An airfield already in the list keeps its settings; a base the mission gives the side outside the campaign's zones (a rear base set in the template) is added by its id, to keep its slots; a side the file does not declare stays without slots. campaign apply has no use here: there is no "between two missions".

    Weather and time come from the server. A persistent campaign runs for weeks: campaign next fixes neither date, start time nor weather, and the mission's name carries no _NoMizedit (Campaign_<campaign>_Mission_01_<title>), so the server's MizEdit preset (with RealWeather) sets them at each load. A multi-mission campaign keeps its fixed conditions and its _NoMizedit.

    The mission's file name¶

    The DCS server browser shows the file name, and Campaign_<campaign>_Mission_01_<title>_<YYYYMMDD>.miz is too long to read there. The date also changes the name at each redeploy, and the server's configuration must then be edited. mission_file gives the file a fixed name, with no date:

    campaign:
  name: Northern Syria
  mode: persistent
  mission_file: Campagne_Oronte     # the build writes Campagne_Oronte.miz

    campaign next writes this name into mission.name and adds mission.date_suffix: false to mission.yaml: the build then writes Campagne_Oronte.miz in the mission folder, and a redeploy just replaces the file. The name is made of ASCII letters, digits, dashes and underscores, with no extension or .miz; campaign validate refuses any other name. Without the key, the name stays Campaign_<campaign>_Mission_01_<title>, dated by the build. The key is refused on a multi-mission campaign: each of its missions needs _NoMizedit in its file name, and a file of its own.

    The briefings are those of a campaign with no mission of the day: the theatre dossier and the kneeboard replace the campaign briefing and the mission briefing.

    campaign next also adds to the mission's ctld-config.yaml what the players carry for the campaign: the "Zone supplies" crate (one per side), the "Spy" troop group (one man) and the "Sappers" group (two men, the demolition team). It writes the players' waypoints, one per zone, and, when it creates the mission folder, its radio plan and its DCS briefing from briefing.yaml — so a mission folder created afresh keeps it; write it there, not in the mission alone. It also enforces the "fog of war" F10 view (mission.forced_options.f10_view: fog_of_war): a pilot sees only the enemies their side has detected, and a garrison's strength is learned by reconnaissance. A view already chosen in mission.yaml is kept.

    No interpreter command in a persistent campaign. A unit named #veafInterpreter["…"] in the Mission Editor spawns its units afresh at every start of the mission: in a persistent campaign, they come back whole after each server restart, their losses forgotten, paid by nobody. What the campaign must hold is declared in campaign.yaml, as a zone with its garrison (an explicit garrison: list when the composition matters): the zone is saved with its losses, and the network rebuilds it at its side's expense. campaign next reports the interpreter commands it finds in the mission.

    What campaign validate and campaign next warn about, each a defect of the first evening flown:

    • a zone at coordinates within 4 km of an airfield, with no kind:: put it on the airfield (at: { airfield: … }), or DCS and the campaign each capture their own object;
    • no AWACS nor tanker in dynamic.support.assets, no dynamic.economy.tanker, or one of them that is no group of the template mission: the players fly without, and the shop has no tanker;
    • no boom tanker (KC-135, KC-10A) or no basket tanker (KC-135MPRS, KC-130, S-3B, IL-78M) among dynamic.support.assets: the F-16 and the A-10, or the F/A-18 and the AV-8B, cannot refuel;
    • a channel of the template's src/presets.yaml named after an airfield of another theatre: write the campaign's frequency plan for its theatre;
    • more long- or medium-range SAMs than allowed, or too close, is an error.

    A waypoint per zone¶

    campaign next writes in src/waypoints.yaml a waypoint per zone of the campaign, at its centre, on the ground, for the players' planes and helicopters, the most strategic first: the zones of the front (connected to a zone of another owner), then the airfields, the factories, the depots and the rest, nearest the players' zones first. A module that holds fewer steerpoints than there are zones keeps the first ones. Each zone keeps its number, the one after the departure point: the F10 label, the situation, the shop, the intelligence and every announcement name it "Taftanaz (WP 7)". The order is the one of the campaign's state when campaign next runs; the build adds BULLSEYE after them. A waypoints.yaml edited by hand is left alone, as for a multi-mission campaign, and the numbers the players read are the ones it holds: a zone it leaves out shows none.

    The radio plan¶

    When it creates the mission folder, campaign next writes the players' radio plan in src/presets.yaml; edited afterwards, it is the mission maker's.

    Radio Channels
    VHF, the airfields' radio 1 Home Base, which each pilot sets to the field taken off from; then the players' side's airfields, with the frequencies DCS gives them on this theatre; then the VEAF flights, on their VHF frequency: a flight tuned to a tanker's UHF still talks on the VHF
    UHF the AWACS, then the tankers of dynamic.support.assets and economy.tanker, on their group's frequency, each named with how it refuels ("Tanker Texaco (boom)", "Tanker Arco (basket)"); Air-Sol, the common air-to-ground frequency (305.0); then the VEAF flights, Archer first

    A channel needs a frequency for the build to keep it on the radio and the kneeboard: Home Base carries a placeholder no airfield of DCS uses (VHF 118.025, UHF 230.025), which the pilot overwrites. The AWACS and the tankers must therefore be in the template before the first campaign next.

    The theatre dossier and the kneeboard¶

    A persistent campaign has no mission of the day: instead of the campaign briefing and the mission briefing, campaign next and campaign briefing write the theatre dossier into missions\mission-01\, <operation>_dossier-de-theatre.pptx, with its two maps (carte-du-reseau.png, carte-des-defenses.png). It is a dated picture of the situation ("État au 17/11/2026"), not an operation order: no objectives, no ATO, no weather. The same dossier is also written as a PDF beside it, <operation>_dossier-de-theatre.pdf, readable without PowerPoint (as every briefing is).

    Page What it says
    Cover the operation, the theatre, the date of the picture
    Strategic situation briefing.yaml: political, economic, enemy course of action
    Network map each zone in its side's colour, a symbol per kind (square airfield, circle outpost, triangle factory, diamond depot), its waypoint number, the connections, the territory tinted by side and the front with its short hatches on each side, as on the F10 map
    The enemy network which factory feeds which zones, what each depot speeds up, the zones already cut off, and the critical nodes: the enemy zones whose capture cuts other zones off from their factory
    Known air defences the long- and medium-range SAMs with their reach drawn as a circle, and what intelligence knows of them ("probable" until a reconnaissance confirms it); ours too
    Axes of effort axes: from briefing.yaml, given as indicative: the situation moves, adapt
    Our means our zones, our airfields, the AWACS and the tankers
    Radio plan the UHF and VHF channels of the radio plan, read from src/presets.yaml
    Player's guide the F10 Campaign menu, the rule for taking a zone, the shop prices
    Rules of engagement briefing.yaml

    The axes of effort are written in briefing.yaml, under an axes: key, each with a title and a text:

    axes:
  - title: Take Menagh and Afrin
    text: Drop troops or supply crates in both neutral towns and hold them for two minutes.
  - title: Cut the red network
    text: The critical nodes of the theatre dossier cut zones off from their factory; taking them weakens everything those zones hold.

    A briefing.yaml written before axes: still works: without that key, the tasks of missions: 1: stand in for it.

    The kneeboard. campaign next also writes four kinds of pages into the mission's src/mission/KNEEBOARD/IMAGES, which the build puts into the .miz and every aircraft shows:

    1. the network map, in the kneeboard's format;
    2. the zone table: waypoint, name and kind, coordinates (DDM and MGRS), elevation in feet, the airfield's VHF/UHF frequencies;
    3. the radio plan;
    4. a cheat sheet: the F10 Campaign menu, the capture rule, the shop prices.

    No weather page (the server makes the weather) and no objectives. These pages are named NN-briefing-campaign-….jpg: campaign next replaces them at every run and leaves the folder's other pages alone. They show the state at the time of campaign next, not the server's save: run campaign next and the build again to refresh them.

    The DCS briefing comes down to the situation (the operation, the mission statement, the strategic situation), the axes of effort, and a pointer to the kneeboard for the map, the coordinates, the frequencies and the cheat sheet.

    Long-range SAMs¶

    A long-range SAM is a zone's own: long_range_sam: true on the zone. Two SA-5 17 nautical miles apart put a whole front under 240 km of missile reach on the first evening flown; campaign validate therefore refuses more than long_range_sams.per_side (2) per side, and two of a side closer than long_range_sams.min_spacing (120 km). Keep them for the key points of the network: a large base, a factory, a depot.

    long_range_sams: { per_side: 2, min_spacing: 120000 }
zones:
  - { name: Aleppo, at: { airfield: Aleppo }, size: airfield, side: red, long_range_sam: true }

    A medium-range SAM is set the same way: medium_range_sam: true on the zone adds a battery <zone> medium-range SAM to its garrison. Red draws an SA-11, an SA-6 or an SA-3 (an SA-6 or an SA-3 in the cold war), blue a Hawk or a NASAMS (a Hawk in the cold war). medium_range_sams caps them per side (per_side, 3) and spaces them (min_spacing, 60 km); both are estimates, to tune after an evening. A sleeping zone that holds one is assumed to reach 40 km: the AI keeps its CAPs and strikes out of that circle, as it does at 75 km for a long-range battery. A zone taken loses its battery and the new owner draws its own, as for the long range.

    medium_range_sams: { per_side: 3, min_spacing: 60000 }
zones:
  - { name: Hama, at: { airfield: Hama }, size: airfield, side: red, medium_range_sam: true }

    The save¶

    The mission writes its state every save_seconds (60 s) and at mission end to:

    <Saved Games>\DCS\Missions\Saves\<campaign name>\persistent.state

    At start it reads that file back: each zone takes back its owner, its garrison with its losses and its capture in progress, a factory its buildings standing or destroyed, an airfield its weapon stocks; the credits, each side's resources and the current intelligence come back too. A zone added to campaign.yaml since starts from its declared state; a zone removed is ignored, and the log says so.

    What is not restored after a restart: the aircraft, helicopters and convoys under way. They are lost; the zones and garrisons are not, and the survivors of a convoy paid from a reserve go back to it. What survives comes home: an assault convoy, a paid spawn still standing or a delivery of the network on its way when the server stopped pay their price back into their side's resources at the next load.

    The file is a Lua table read with no access to anything: it cannot run code. The server's MissionScripting.lua must leave io and lfs open, as the VEAF servers do. Without them the campaign runs anyway, but does not save, and says so once.

    Reset: F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign → Reset the campaign (admin). The campaign starts again from its declared state at the next load of the mission; a victory or a defeat does the same.

    The block for the web map¶

    Every save adds a sitac block to the same file, made for a map read outside DCS: VEAF's web sitac (foothold-sitac) reads it to draw the campaign as it draws Foothold. The mission never reads this block back: it is computed again at every save.

    sitac = {
  theatre = "Syria", campaign = "Bouclier de l'Oronte", updated = 5432.1, -- simulation time, in seconds
  zones = {
    { name = "Minakh", waypoint = 3, kind = "airfield", lat = 36.52, lon = 37.04, owner = "blue",
      garrison = { alive = 9, total = 12 }, awake = true },
    { name = "Tal Rifaat", kind = "outpost", lat = 36.47, lon = 37.10, owner = "neutral",
      capture = { side = "blue", seconds = 42 }, awake = false },
    { name = "Kafr Naya", kind = "factory", lat = 36.41, lon = 37.02, owner = "red",
      garrison = { alive = 5, total = 6 }, structures = { standing = 2, total = 3 }, awake = false },
  },
  connections = { { "Minakh", "Tal Rifaat" }, { "Tal Rifaat", "Kafr Naya" } },
  convoys = { { side = "blue", from = "Minakh", to = "Tal Rifaat", units = 4 } },
  sides = { blue = { credits = 1915, resources = 600 }, red = { resources = 250 } },
  players = {
    { coalition = "blue", unitType = "A-10C_2", playerName = "Zip", latitude = 36.50, longitude = 37.01, altitude = 3048 },
  },
  playerStats = {
    Zip = { Points = 420, Air = 1, SAM = 2, ["Ground Units"] = 7, Infantry = 3, Helo = 0, Structure = 1, Deaths = 1,
      ["Warehouse delivery"] = 2 },
  },
  ejectedPilots = { { playerName = "Paluche", latitude = 36.45, longitude = 37.08, altitude = 412, lostCredits = 15 } },
  missions = {
    { title = "Capture Tal Rifaat (300 cr.)", description = "", isRunning = true, isEscortMission = false,
      kind = "capture", zone = "Tal Rifaat", reward = 300 },
    { title = "Air superiority (200 cr.)", description = "1/3", isRunning = true, isEscortMission = false,
      kind = "air", reward = 200 },
  },
  support = {
    { name = "Arco", kind = "tanker_boom", side = "blue", latitude = 36.20, longitude = 36.60, altitude = 7620,
      frequency = 252.5, tacan = "51Y", alive = true },
    { name = "Overlord", alive = false },
  },
}
    • zones follows the order of campaign.yaml; kind is airfield, outpost, factory or depot; lat/lon are the zone's centre, in decimal degrees.
    • garrison gives only the counts of units alive and in total, never their type: the composition stays hidden until the zone is reconnoitred, and the map is public. A neutral zone has none.
    • capture is there only during a capture; structures, only for a factory or a depot that has buildings.
    • awake is false for a sleeping zone.
    • convoys keeps only the assault convoys still on the road; units counts their vehicles alive.
    • sides gives the players' side's credits and each side's resources, when they are counted.

    The next five lists use the field names of Foothold's save, so that the sitac shows them as it shows Foothold's. They are as fresh as the save: one every state_write_seconds (60 s) of simulation, and none while the server is paused.

    • players: each player in a slot, both sides, with his side, aircraft and position (altitude in metres).
    • playerStats: the ranking, by pilot name. Points is the total of the credits he earned; Air, Helo, SAM (all the ground-based air defence), Ground Units, Infantry and Structure count what he destroyed; Deaths his deaths, Warehouse delivery his weapon deliveries. These figures are kept in the save and start from zero with the campaign. A capture is credited to no pilot: it does not show in the ranking. A pilot of the players' side is in it as soon as he takes a slot, at zero if he has earned nothing yet.
    • ejectedPilots: the players' side's pilots on the ground the CSAR waits to rescue, AI included; lostCredits gives the credits he carried, lost at the ejection (a rescue does not give them back). A pilot picked up leaves the list.
    • missions: the task board, with the title the F10 menu gives; description gives an air superiority task's progress; kind, zone and reward on top of Foothold's.
    • support: the players' side's AWACS and tankers, where they fly, with their kind (awacs, tanker_boom, tanker_basket, tanker_both, tanker), their frequency in MHz and their TACAN; one destroyed has only its name and alive = false.

    The enemy's units, zone by zone, are never there: that is the intelligence rule.

    Sleeping zones¶

    Away from the players, a zone has no unit in the world: its garrison is only a line of the save, losses included. It comes back exactly as it was:

    • always, when it is within front_depth (40 km) of the front line drawn on the map, or connected to a zone of another side, or targeted by a sortie: an enemy going round finds someone there;
    • when an enemy player's aircraft comes within wake_radius (60 km), or within its air defence's reach plus reach_wake_margin (20 km) when that reach is longer;
    • when an enemy aircraft in the air, AI included, comes within its air defence's reach plus reach_wake_margin: a long-range SAM therefore wakes much earlier than a zone with guns only;
    • when an enemy ground group that is no garrison (a convoy, troops dropped, a spawn) comes within ground_wake_radius (10 km).

    Only a zone's enemies wake it; vehicles placed in the editor and aircraft not yet activated wake nothing. A defence's reach is its missiles', read from DCS while the zone is awake and kept in the save; a zone never seen awake has an assumed reach (75 km with a long-range SAM, 20 km otherwise). It disappears when nobody has come near for sleep_seconds (600 s), except near the front. Awake, a zone keeps its garrison sleep_margin (20 km) farther than it wakes: a player hovering around 60 km does not make it disappear and appear again. Asleep for less than wake_rest_seconds (300 s), it wakes for the front, a sortie or a player only, not for an AI aircraft passing by.

    At most max_awake_zones (12) zones of the rear are awake at once; the front and the targets of sorties do not count. When the cap is reached, a zone wanted by a player takes the place of a zone only AI wants, or of a zone wanted by a player farther away; between zones wanted by AI, the first keeps its place, so that passing aircraft do not make zones appear and disappear in a loop. Only one zone wakes per 10-second beat, so the whole front does not appear in one go. Its garrison comes group by group, about wake_units_per_beat (10) units per beat, its long-range battery last, a group never split (a launcher apart from its radar does not fire): Hama's 42 units in one go froze the server; the garrison of a zone just taken comes the same way.

    A front that moves on its own¶

    • Counter-attacks: every attack_seconds (900 s), a side listed in attacks (red by default) sends an assault convoy against the weakest enemy zone it borders. Only those sides send convoys on their own toward a neutral zone too; the players' side buys theirs (F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign → Assaults), and no automatic convoy leaves for a zone a convoy of the same side already drives to.
    • As many convoys as there are players to stop them: a side has one assault convoy on the road per players_per_convoy (4) enemy players armed to stop one, rounded up, at least 1, at most max_convoys (3); assaults on a neutral zone count too, and wait for a free place. A player counts in a slot, airborne or on the ground, carrying bombs, rockets or air-to-ground missiles (Maverick, Hellfire, APKWS…); a fighter armed for air-to-air only, or a SEAD aircraft carrying anti-radiation missiles only, does not, nor does the gun. A convoy already on the road stays there when players leave: the count only decides whether a new convoy leaves. On 2026-11-20, with three players, red kept three convoys on the road all evening.
    • A counter-attack goes where the players are not: a zone is defended when one of those players is within defended_radius (30 km) of its centre; the counter-attack takes the weakest undefended zone, and the weakest of all only when every one is defended. The players hear of the convoy as before and can turn back: the rule makes them choose between defending and attacking, it does not hide the attack.
    • An assault convoy drives at assault_speed (40 km/h), without dispersing under fire; at its departure, dcs.log gives the length of its road, the time it needs and when it will be retired, then every convoy_report_seconds (300 s) its distance to its target and whether it moves on, stands still or turns back. Under aircraft it drives on; if it falls back before a stronger ground force for fall_back_seconds (120 s) without taking its road again, its assault is over: it is retired.
    • A convoy that will not arrive frees its place: once its target is its side's (a helicopter took it first), when it falls back, or after the time its road needs (half as long again as expected, convoy_timeout, 2,700 s, at least), it is taken off the map and the price of its survivors goes back to its side; on 2026-11-17, at 20 km/h on roads of 40 to 60 km, the 45 minutes retired every convoy before it arrived. Never in front of the players: a convoy to retire stays in play while a player is within wake_radius of it, or it was in contact in the last retire_quiet_seconds (300 s) — a shot fired or taken, an enemy newly in sight, but not its own attempts to drive onto an enemy it does not reach; its losses count, and only its survivors when it leaves are given back. On 2026-11-19, Aleppo's assault on Menagh vanished under a player's fire.
    • The other side is told at once of a convoy leaving, and sees its axis on the map.
    • Resupply: a delivery to a damaged friendly zone brings back the dead units of its first damaged group, paid from the side's resource stock at the price their spawn would cost, cheapest first; what the stock cannot pay stays dead (a side whose resources are not counted gets the whole group back). Deliveries are: a "Zone supplies" crate set down in the zone, CTLD 2 troops deployed in the zone (the spy team and the JTAC teams excepted), a supply helicopter landed in it.
    • AI helicopters: every supply_seconds (600 s), a side listed in supply (red by default) sends a helicopter (helicopter, a Mi-8MT for red, a UH-1H for blue) to a neutral zone it borders, which it takes by landing there, or else to its most damaged zone, which it resupplies; max_helicopters (2) in the air at most, and one that has not landed in time is removed: its route's flight at 40 m/s, times 1.5, plus 5 minutes for the start-up and the landing, and at least helicopter_timeout (1,200 s); dcs.log says it when it leaves (… leaves [Bassel Al-Assad] to resupply [Jisr al-Shughur], 57 km, timeout 41 min). On 2026-11-22 the fixed 20 minutes removed on its way every Mi-8 sent 57 km away, and the next one left at once. The other side is told when it leaves, to intercept it. One that lands again away from its zone has failed at once, and dcs.log says where it landed. In an airfield zone it lands on the field; in any other zone (a town, a point), on the clear spot nearest the centre, within 60 % of its radius: sent to Afrin's centre, four Mi-8 turned back at once.
    • The AI remembers what fails: after route_failures (2) helicopters or assault convoys of a side failed in a row to the same zone, that side sends nothing of the kind there for route_failure_seconds (3,600 s); a success clears the count.

    Enemy air¶

    • CAP: the enemy keeps one CAP up per air.cap_per_players (3) players of the opposition level, over its front zone nearest our fighters. That level follows the fighters in the air: the players carrying a radar-guided air-to-air missile (the air_to_air follow, set by default when the mission follows nothing else). Helicopters and attack airplanes (A-10, Su-25, AV-8B…) do not count: when no fighter flies, no CAP takes off. Its patrol circle (40 km at most) stops 15 km short of our zones' air defence reach; when even a 5 km circle would be in their reach, it takes the next front zone, and does not fly when none suits: it does not throw itself at our SAMs. It engages our combat airplanes; a helicopter or an attack airplane it detects is engaged only on a draw of air.cap_helicopter_chance (25 %), made once per aircraft, as Foothold's InvisibleA10 option.
    • Strikes: every air.strike_seconds (900 s), a CAS strike (on the zone with the most units) or a SEAD strike (on the one with the most air defence), in turn, on a zone bordering the enemy's territory. A CAS strike leaves out a zone under a measured air defence of 15 km or more (a Hawk, an SA-6), the zone's own or a neighbour's: that is the SEAD's job. A strike opens fire only on its zone's ground units (ROE open fire, not weapons free), even with air-to-air missiles on board: on 2026-11-22 a Su-25, weapons free, shot the AWACS down with its R-60M.
    • They take off from the nearest enemy airfield, hot on free stands (shelters first, open stands after; a large aircraft, bomber or transport, never in a shelter), or from the runway when there are not enough; no airfield, no sortie. So do the CAP, the BARCAP and the Hunter. Nothing takes off from an airfield within takeoff_threat_range (40 km) of an enemy air defence whose reach was measured (a Patriot would shoot the aircraft down while they climb); the reach DCS gives is the missile's against a high target, much longer than the range at which the SAM sees an aircraft taking off, hence this cap, an estimation to tune. No aeroplane of the AI leaves a field whose longest runway is shorter than min_runway_length (1,500 m), and one flight at a time leaves a field's runway: on 2026-11-17 fifteen aircraft sent from Taftanaz piled up on one spot of its strip and never took off. A flight still on the ground takeoff_timeout (600 s) after its spawn is taken away, its price back in the stock, and its field sends no aeroplane for grounded_seconds (3,600 s). A SEAD flight takes off all the same: putting that defence out is its job, and what it destroys frees the field for the strikes. Otherwise the sortie leaves from another airfield, or does not leave, and dcs.log says so ([CAMPAIGN DECISION] nothing of red takes off from [Senaki]: it is under the air defence of [Kobuleti]). air.max_flights (4) flights in the air at most per side.
    • Hunter: a player flying a fighter on a kill streak (between 8 and 16 on the ground, or between 4 and 8 in the air, drawn once per life) then has a 10 % chance at every kill to have two enemy fighters scrambled at that player, whose side is told.

    Testing the balance without a server¶

    The economy simulator plays the Oronte campaign with the real campaign code (its four modules), without DCS, as Iron Gall's pacing report does. It advances time beat by beat; combat is not simulated: what a team of virtual players does is drawn from rates (kills an hour, captures an evening, ejections), set on the server logs of 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-19. The red AI plays with its real decision code: production, CAP and strikes, helicopters, convoys, reinforcements at their units' price.

    From the repository:

    poetry run campaign-sim report 9 5 --csv economy.csv

    It plays 9 h (three evenings of 3 h), on 5 seeds, three teams (occasional, average, expert) with four shop strategies: buy nothing, buy the cheapest item not bought yet, save for a Hawk, save for a Patriot. For each, the median run gives the hour of each item's first purchase, the credits earned and spent hour by hour, the red stock, and the hour from which red can no longer pay a SEAD or a raid; --csv writes the hour-by-hour rows. poetry run campaign-sim calibrate checks the rates: the average team must earn about 2,000 credits an evening with the rewards of before 2026-11-20, which the server measured.

    The test test/lua/test_campaignEconomySim.lua keeps three bounds, measured on 2026-11-20: the average team buys its first CAP between 1 h and 3 h of play (2 h 30 measured), its Hawk between 1 h 30 and 6 h saving for it (2 h 50), and red can pay a SEAD for 45 minutes at least (1 h). A price or a reward that breaks this pacing turns the CI red. What the simulator does not play is written in test/lua/campaign_sim.lua: the players' zones take no damage, nothing happens between two evenings.

    Intelligence¶

    An enemy zone's garrison is unknown to the players ("strength unknown" on the map) until it is reconnoitred, for intel.seconds (one hour):

    • by an overflight: a player's aircraft within the zone's circle, less than intel.overflight_altitude (1,500 m) above the ground;
    • by a JTAC drone or intel bought in the shop;
    • by a spy team: the "Spy" CTLD 2 troop group deployed within intel.spy_range (5 km) of an enemy zone walks into it on its own, and reveals it on arrival.

  - { name: Samtredia, at: { lat: 42.165, lon: 42.335 }, size: outpost, side: red, kind: depot }

    A factory's buildings are drawn once, at the first start: factory.buildings (3) among factory.types (Workshop A, Tech combine, Tech hangar A), in a row 60 m apart on open ground in the zone. They are statics: they stay in the world even while the zone sleeps. The map counts those standing, "factory 2/3". They are destroyed by bombs or by the demolition team; a factory is also captured like any other zone, and what stands of it then works for its new side. A delivery (crate, troops, helicopter) to a factory whose garrison is whole rebuilds one building.

    A depot has buildings too, drawn the same way: network.depot_buildings (3) among network.depot_types (Warehouse, Fuel tank, Tank); the map says "depot 2/3". While one of its buildings stands it is a depot: it keeps its store, shortens the hops and may supply itself. Its buildings all destroyed, its store burns with them and it is only an ordinary node of the network: supplied like any other zone, from a factory or another depot. It is a depot again only after network.depot_rebuild_seconds (2,700 s), all its buildings rebuilt at once for the side holding the zone: time enough to destroy its garrison and take it. No delivery rebuilds a depot's building. To take a depot: destroy its buildings first, or it repairs its garrison itself; then its garrison, before the rebuild.

    The AI's resources¶

    Each side has one stock of resource points (resources.start, 1,000), saved with the campaign, and everything the AI puts into play is paid from it at the price of its units: the garrison of a captured zone, a reinforcement by the network, an assault convoy, a supply helicopter, a CAP, a strike, the Hunter, a bomber raid. An empty stock sends nothing: a sortie leaves only if the stock stays above resources.reserve (100) once it is paid, so that the AI keeps what it needs to rebuild its zones. A captured zone its side cannot pay gets a token garrison, and so does a zone taken by helicopters alone. A paid spawn can take the stock below zero: the AI then pays for nothing, sortie nor reinforcement, until production brings it back up.

    Production is added at every beat: a background production, resources.background_per_hour (60 points an hour), even with no factory, enough to just about hold a base; and each factory, factory.per_hour (180) times the share of its buildings standing. These three values were raised after the first evening flown: with 500, 30 and 120, red had nothing left after 1 h 20, no CAP, strike nor raid.

    The supply network¶

    A side's factories feed its zones hop by hop, along the connections, and only through the side's zones: an enemy or neutral zone cuts the path, and the rest of the network is served by a detour, later.

    • A hop from one zone to the next takes network.hop_seconds (600 s); a hop that touches a depot half of it: factory → base → base is two full hops, factory → depot → base one.
    • A depot keeps a store of network.depot_store (4) deliveries, full while a factory reaches it: cut from its factories, it still feeds the zones around it until it runs dry. Its buildings destroyed, it keeps nothing and shortens no hop.
    • A damaged zone, of either side, orders a delivery, paid from the resources at the order at the price of the units it is missing; network.max_deliveries (3) deliveries of a side on their way at once. After the zone's delay, the delivery brings back the dead units of its first damaged group, cheapest first and up to what it paid: a unit killed since the order stays dead. The units brought back appear in their places; the living ones do not move. A zone under fire — a unit lost less than network.under_fire_seconds (600 s) ago — is not reinforced: the delivery waits. A zone lost meanwhile receives nothing; a delivery with nothing left to repair, or what it did not spend, goes back to the stock. Players who fly logistics go faster: a crate, troops or a helicopter reinforce at once, without waiting for the network; the units brought back are paid from the stock, at their spawn price (see Resupply in A front that moves on its own).
    • A zone cut off from every factory and depot is still served, from the background production, after network.isolated_seconds (3,600 s).

    F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign → Supply network gives the players their stock, their production, and for each of their zones its delay or "cut off".

    Paid spawns¶

    In a persistent campaign, every VEAF spawn command a person drops as a marker — _spawn, -armor, -cap, a convoy, a crate, game masters included — is paid:

    • in credits when it spawns units for the players' side;
    • from the resources of the other side otherwise;
    • at the price of the units it made, one by one, on the resources' scale: a random armoured platoon costs what it drew, two tanks and an IFV are 6 + 6 + 4 = 16 points;
    • the players' side pays economy.credits_per_point (6) credits a point: those 16 points are 96 credits; a -sam costs more since 2026-11-19, a short-range SAM being worth 25 points (150 credits) instead of 8;
    • the price is known only once the units are drawn: the spawn therefore always goes through whole — multiplier included — and is paid its exact price, into debt if need be;
    • a side at zero or in debt spawns nothing more: the command is refused before the spawn, with the balance, until the balance is above zero again; a gain pays the debt off first.

    Smoke, flares, drawings, destroy, teleport and mission master stay free: they put no unit into play. A batch alias (-arty1) is paid like its aliases: its battery is charged, though the password is checked only once. What a script spawns (a combat zone, a command the interpreter reads from a name placed in the editor, the campaign's own sorties) is no person's action and is not paid here.

    Reserved commands¶

    Some commands would be cheating in a persistent campaign: it reserves them to the admin level (the one of the VEAF pilots file), whatever their level elsewhere.

    Reserved to admins What they do
    -shell, -cesar, -flak, _spawn bomb explosions wherever one likes
    -destroy, _destroy destroy the units around the marker
    -tanker, -tankerhere, _teleport, _move … teleport teleport a group or the tanker
    -zonestart, -zonestop, -airstart, -airstop start or stop a combat zone or an air wave
    -send, -play, _radio broadcast a message or a sound to the whole server
    -light, _spawn flare free illumination, which the shop sells

    -cas is turned off: a CAS training zone makes no sense in a campaign. Everything else keeps its module's level: smoke, signals, named points, beacons, drawings, the tanker's altitude, the commands of an artillery battery already placed. The same rule holds for a command a pilot sends from afar. A mission with security off (a test copy) lets everything through, -cas excepted.

    The demolition team¶

    The CTLD 2 troop group "Sappers" (demolition.troops), dropped by the players' side, walks to the enemy factory whose buildings are within demolition.range (5 km) of where it was dropped. Within 200 m of the buildings, it plants its charges on every one standing, says so, and walks back to its drop point. The charges blow demolition.fuse (120 s) later, even if the team was killed meanwhile. Each building destroyed is worth economy.demolition (100) credits, as much as a pilot gets for one, and the factory makes that much less. With no enemy factory in range, the team says so and stays where it is.

    Weapon stocks¶

    The weapon stocks of each airfield of the campaign, read at every save, are set back as they were at the restart; an unlimited stock is left as it is. The campaign makes no warehouse limited or unlimited: that is the Mission Editor's choice.

    The players move stock from one field to another, in a helicopter or a transport aircraft:

    1. on the ground at an airfield of the coalition, F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign → Warehouse → Load weapons takes warehouse.load (10) of each weapon the warehouse holds (fewer when it has fewer);
    2. on the ground at another airfield of the coalition, Unload weapons puts in exactly what is aboard, and earns economy.warehouse_delivery (50) credits, once per airfield in each flight;
    3. Cargo tells what is aboard.

    The cargo is lost with the aircraft, and with the server: it is not saved. warehouse.enabled: false removes the menu and the restoring of the stocks.

    Strategic bombers¶

    Every bombers.seconds (3,600 s), the enemy sends a combined raid at the players' zone with the most to hit: a factory first, else the zone with the most units, among those one of its airfields can reach. Everything takes off from the enemy airfield nearest the target that is at least bombers.min_distance (80 km) from it, to leave the players time to intercept, like every campaign aircraft:

    1. a BARCAP holds the axis, out of the reach of every enemy air defence (see the BARCAP);
    2. a SEAD flight, at the zone whose air defence covers the target (when one does), and the bomber meet at a hold point on the target's axis, the first point out of the defences' reach plus 15 km (over their airfield when there is none);
    3. once both circle there, climbed to their altitude (within 1,000 m) and within 15 km of the point (a holding circle's radius), the SEAD pushes to the target, and the bomber bombers.package_delay (30 s) behind it, on the same axis; when the SEAD was shot down on the way, the bomber pushes alone. Neither circles the hold point longer than 20 minutes.

    The bomber flies at the SEAD's speed, to stay behind it, and drops its whole load in one pass, in a 100 m carpet on the target; it does not react to threats nor jettison its bombs: it presses on to the target. After the target, it flies 20 km back along the axis it came in on, which its SEAD cleared, before it turns home. With no defence to put out, no SEAD and no hold: the bomber flies straight to the target.

    No escort by default: the BARCAP holds the approach, and the last leg is the players' interception window; bombers.escort: true gives it one back. The package is paid whole, from the resources, or does not leave. The players are told at once, and the task board posts an intercept task. One raid of a side in the air at a time.

    The players buy the same package in the shop: Bomber raid, on an enemy zone.

    The bombers are declared like the other aircraft, role bomber:

    dynamic:
  air:
    red:
      bomber: { type: Tu-22M3, loadout: "FAB-500*33", count: 1 }
    blue:
      bomber: { type: B-1B, loadout: "Mk-84*24", count: 1 }

    AWACS and tankers¶

    The AWACS and tankers named in support.assets (assets of the mission, with an orbit in the Mission Editor) are held out of the enemy's air defence by the veafSupport module, which campaign next turns on in mission.yaml with these settings; the campaign hands it the reach of its zones, asleep or awake, and its front:

    • on the line from their Mission Editor orbit to the enemy zone nearest it, at the first point out of the reach of every enemy air defence plus support.margin (30 km), never ahead of their side's own zone nearest that enemy;
    • an AWACS, besides, at least support.awacs_standoff (100 km) from every enemy zone, even one held by MANPADS alone: on 2026-11-22 a zone reaching 4 km left it 35 km away; the tankers stay as near the front as the reach allows;
    • out at once, with support.retreat_margin (50 km), when a reach comes over its orbit; closer only after 30 minutes of calm and for a step of 30 km at least (support.advance_seconds, support.min_advance), so a SAM that wakes and sleeps does not make it shuttle; its leg (support.leg, 30 nm) laid across the axis;
    • its F10 marks (leg and label, for its side) redrawn at each move;
    • each move told to its side: the new orbit in bearing and range from the bullseye, frequency unchanged.

    A persistent campaign holds two tankers, a boom (a KC-135) and a basket (a KC-135MPRS or a KC-130 for blue), each placed out of reach like the AWACS, and both sold in the shop.

    dynamic:
  support:
    assets: [ "AWACS Magic", "Tanker Texaco", "Tanker Arco" ]
  economy:
    tanker: [ "Tanker Texaco", "Tanker Arco" ]

    The reach is the one the BARCAP uses: measured on the missiles while a zone is awake, assumed for a zone never seen awake.

    SAM sites under Skynet¶

    The SAM sites of both sides are commanded by Skynet, VEAF's air defence network: campaign next turns the SKYNET module on in mission.yaml, and the campaign declares each SAM group it spawns to its side's network.

    What a pilot sees:

    • a site that sees an anti-radiation missile coming often turns its radar off, then on again once the missile has gone;
    • a dark site keeps a small defence radius of its own (Skynet's "last line of defence"): it lights up when an aircraft enters that radius;
    • with no early-warning radar (EWR), and the campaign has none, a site keeps its own radar on, as before.

    What runs under Skynet:

    • a garrison's SAM made of several vehicles (a launcher that fires on another vehicle's radar, its radars, its command post): it is drawn into a group of its own, <zone> SAM (for instance Hatay SAM, the Hawk of a blue level-5 zone);
    • the long- and medium-range batteries (<zone> long-range SAM, <zone> medium-range SAM);
    • the zone upgrades that carry a missile launcher: Hawk, NASAMS, IRIS-T SLM, Patriot, Rapier.

    The short-range weapons (Gepard, Avenger, Stinger, Shilka, Tunguska…) stay in the garrison's main group, with DCS's AI: Skynet commands a whole group, and would turn the whole group dark with its SAM. A save from before this change is split the same way at load, with no new draw: each unit keeps its type, position, heading and state, and the split is written at the next save. The SAM group wakes whole, last, like the batteries.

    dcs.log records each declaration, with or without debug: [CAMPAIGN SKYNET] [Hatay SAM] declared to blue iads. A group spawned again under its name (a zone woken again, a SAM brought back by a delivery) replaces its old site in the network (its old site replaced). skynet.enabled: false leaves the SAM sites to DCS's AI, as before.

    dynamic:
  skynet:
    enabled: false

    Territory¶

    Each zone holds the part of the map nearer to it than to any other zone (its Voronoi cell), tinted on the F10 map in its side's colour, grey for a neutral zone; a dark line runs between two cells of different sides: the front, edged on each side with short slanted hatches in the side's colour, plain to see where the tint is faint. The map is redrawn only when a zone changes hands. territory.margin (70 km) extends the cells beyond the outer zones; territory.hatch_spacing (3,000 m) and territory.hatch_length (2,500 m) set the hatches' spacing and length; territory.enabled: false removes the tint.

    The debugging log¶

    While the campaign is being tuned, dynamic.debug (on by default) writes in dcs.log what the campaign does, in lines easy to search for:

    Tag What it says
    [CAMPAIGN DECISION] every decision of the AI and its reason: a sortie sent, or not (no target, no airfield, no room left, nothing to pay with — with the price and the stock); a raid's package; a refusal examined again at every beat is told once, until its reason changes
    [CAMPAIGN FLIGHT] the fate of every campaign aircraft: a weapon released (with its distance to the aim, the nearest zone and what it has left), a kill or a loss and by whom, a landing (on an airfield, or in the open near a zone), a raid leaving its hold; a wreck crushing the scenery is not a kill
    [CAMPAIGN AIRDEF] every debug_status_seconds (300 s): for each zone, its air defence units and the missiles they have left, empty launchers included
    [CAMPAIGN SUPPLY] the network's deliveries ordered, arrived, lost
    [CAMPAIGN ZONE] zones captured and turned neutral
    [CAMPAIGN SHOP] each purchase in the shop: the item, the zone, the price and the balance; for a zone upgrade, the group placed and its units
    [CAMPAIGN STATUS] every debug_status_seconds (300 s): per side, zones, stock and production, deliveries on their way, flights up, raid out, and the players' credits

    debug: false turns it off, once the campaign is trusted.

    The credits earned are written whether debug is on or not:

    • one line CREDITS <pilot> +<amount> (<reason>) per gain — kill, kill_held, landed, rescue, warehouse for a pilot; capture, delivery, task, demolition under team;
    • a CREDITS SUMMARY at the mission's end, and when the last player leaves his slot (he leaves it, quits the server from his cockpit, dies, ejects or crashes): what each earned since the previous summary, and the side's total; DCS pauses the mission in the second the last player quits the server, hence this summary written at the event; failing that, it is written at the first beat with nobody left in a slot;
    • at the same moment, an ECONOMY SUMMARY block covering the session, from the first beat a player sits in a slot to the summary, to tune the prices on what is really earned and spent: the session's length; then, for the players' credits and each side's resources (when they are counted), the balance at the start and at the end, what was earned and what was spent, by kind, each with its amount and its count; for the credits, the gains (kill_ground, kill_sam, kill_air, kill_building, rescue, capture, delivery, task…) and the spending (shop <item>, upgrade <upgrade>, spawn, shop assault); for the resources, the production, the refunds (refund) and the spending (flight, helicopter, convoy, garrison, reinforcement at the units' price, raid, spawn);
    ECONOMY SUMMARY (nobody left in a slot): 180 min of play

ECONOMY SUMMARY blue credits earned 2000: capture 1200 (x6), kill_air 500 (x10), kill_ground 300 (x30)

    Checking a mission in game¶

    F10 → Other → VEAF → Campaign → Self-check (admin) prints one OK or KO line per mechanism: zones placed, save read and written, outcome, dormancy, captures, factories, territory, both sides' resources, network (deliveries and zones cut off), paid spawns, sappers, warehouses, helicopters, air, bombers, credits, tasks, intelligence. A KO line is enough to know where to look.
